This happened over about four months and I'm still a little proud of it.

I work in an office with assigned parking spots. Nothing fancy, just a numbered spot per person, in your lease, paid for. Mine is spot 14. It's close to the door, which matters because I have a bad knee and also because I was there first and earned the good spot through the ancient ritual of "being hired in 2019."

Enter Greg. Greg started last year. Greg drives a massive lifted truck that he is, I cannot stress this enough, obsessed with. Greg's assigned spot is 31, which is far from the door, by the dumpsters. And Greg decided that simply would not do.

So Greg started parking in MY spot. Spot 14. Just took it. First time, I figured it was a mistake, left a polite note. Second time, another note. Third time he left a note back on MY car (which I'd had to park in HIS spot by the dumpsters) that said "possession is 9/10ths lol." Lol.

I went to facilities. Facilities said they "don't get involved in spot disputes" and suggested I "talk it out." I talked it out. Greg laughed and said "just park in 31, what's the big deal, you're young." I have a bad knee. He knew about the knee.

So I stopped talking.

I did some quiet research. Turns out our building's spots are tied to the lease, and there's a clause about unauthorized vehicles being subject to towing at the owner's expense — but facilities won't enforce it unless the registered spot-holder formally requests it in writing each time, which is why nobody bothers. It's a hassle. You have to email, attach a photo, confirm it's not your own guest, etc. Nobody does it.

I started doing it. Every single time.

I set a reminder on my phone. Every morning I'd arrive, and if Greg's truck was in spot 14, I'd take a photo, fill out the form, and email facilities the request to tow an unauthorized vehicle from my paid spot. I was unfailingly polite. "Hi, unauthorized vehicle in my assigned spot 14 again, requesting removal per section whatever, photo attached, thanks so much!"

The first tow, Greg was FURIOUS. Came storming in asking who reported him. I said, honestly, "I requested removal of an unauthorized vehicle from my paid spot, like facilities told me to." He couldn't argue because it was literally the process they'd pointed me to.

He paid the tow fee (it's not cheap for a truck that size). And then — this is the Greg of it all — he parked there AGAIN two days later, I think to prove a point. Towed again. Another fee.

This went on. He'd test it every week or two, certain I'd eventually give up because filling the form out is annoying. But here's the thing: it took me ninety seconds, and watching a tow truck wrestle that ridiculous lifted monstrosity onto its bed was worth so much more than ninety seconds to me. I started looking FORWARD to seeing his truck in my spot.

By month three he'd racked up something like five tows. By month four he gave up and parked in spot 31, by the dumpsters, where his lease says he should. He doesn't make eye contact with me anymore.

The kicker: facilities, having processed all my polite, perfectly-documented requests, apparently decided the form was too much overhead, and they've now installed actual signage and a real enforcement policy for the whole lot. Several other people have thanked me. I'm a folk hero in the break room. Greg eats lunch in his truck. By the dumpsters.

Short setup, long payoff. My roommate is a "borrower." Not of money — of food. Specifically, my food, and most specifically, my coffee.

I'm particular about coffee. It's my one nice thing. I buy good beans, I grind them fresh, it's a whole morning ritual that keeps me sane. My roommate drinks coffee maybe twice a month, doesn't own any, and just... uses mine. Which would be fine if she asked, or replaced it, or even acknowledged it. But she doesn't. The bag just empties faster than I'm drinking it, and when I mentioned it she said "it's just coffee, don't be weird about it." Don't be weird about my own coffee that I bought.

I tried labeling. Ignored. I tried hiding the bag. She found it. I tried asking her to chip in for a shared bag and she "doesn't drink it enough to pay for it" — which is hilarious, because she drinks exactly enough of MINE.

So I stopped fighting it and got scientific instead.

I bought two bags. One bag of my actual good beans, which now lives in my bedroom in a sealed container, for me. And one decoy bag that stays in the kitchen where the coffee always was. The decoy bag is decaf. Not just any decaf — the cheapest, most aggressively mediocre decaf I could find, decanted into the nice bag so it looks identical to my real beans.

She has been drinking exclusively cheap decaf for two months and complaining, constantly, that she's "so tired lately" and "the coffee doesn't even seem to be working anymore." She has theories. She thinks she might be "building a tolerance to caffeine." She googled it. She bought a sleep tracker.

I make my real coffee in my room with a little french press now, like a raccoon hoarding treasure, and it is the best coffee I have ever had in my life, every single morning, seasoned with the knowledge that she is downstairs drinking the saddest decaf known to man and blaming her circadian rhythm.

The kicker: she recently announced she's "cutting back on coffee since it stopped doing anything for her" and switched to tea. My real beans are safe forever. She solved my problem for me and thanked herself for the wellness journey.
