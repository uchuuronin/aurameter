She’d set alarms at 5:30 AM, snooze them ten times, then not even get up. She’d FaceTime loudly without headphones. She’d leave wet towels on my chair because “your side has better airflow.”

I tried talking. She said shared living required flexibility.

Then finals week happened.

She invited three friends over at midnight to “quietly decompress.” They were not quiet. They watched reality TV, ate chips, and whispered in that fake whisper that’s louder than talking.

I had an exam at 8 AM.

The next morning, I didn’t yell. I didn’t report her.

I simply became very flexible.

She slept until noon most days, so at 8:30 AM after my exam, I returned and began my new wellness routine: cheerful morning affirmations played on speaker while making instant coffee.

Not loud enough to violate rules. Just loud enough to make sleeping through it annoying.

“Today is full of possibilities.”
“I welcome peace into my space.”
“My boundaries are valid.”

Day three, she asked if I could use headphones.

I said shared living required flexibility.

She moved the hangouts to the lounge.

My grades survived.

My fiancé’s family has a cabin they all use, and every year there’s a big summer weekend there. This was my first time going.

Before the trip, his sister sent a packing list and said everyone contributes food. I asked what to bring. She said desserts were covered, but I could bring “something simple.”

So I brought fruit, chips, and drinks.

When we arrived, I realized “something simple” apparently meant I was supposed to intuitively understand their 40-year family meal rotation. His mother made comments all weekend:
“Oh, we usually do homemade.”
“Store-bought is fine for some people.”
“Don’t worry, we’ll teach you.”

Then at dinner she told a story about my fiancé’s ex making fresh cinnamon rolls for everyone one year.

I smiled and said, “That’s sweet. Did she also get a handbook or was she born knowing cabin law?”

The table went quiet.

His mom said she was just sharing a memory. I said I was just asking a question.

Later my fiancé said I embarrassed him. I told him I was tired of being tested on traditions nobody explained.

UPDATE:
Apparently his sister texted him saying I “failed the vibe check.”

I replied in the family chat:
“Totally fair. For next year, please send the rubric.”

No response yet.

My ex-friend ran a group chat like a customer service department.

Every message needed a reaction. If you didn’t respond quickly, she’d send:
“Guess nobody cares.”
Or:
“Cool, I’ll remember this energy.”

She’d trauma-dump at 1 AM, then get mad if people didn’t answer until morning.

The final straw was her birthday dinner. She sent 86 messages about the restaurant, dress code, vibes, and seating. Then she accused me of “not matching her excitement” because I only heart-reacted instead of typing a full response.

So I matched her energy.

For one week, I responded to everything with maximum corporate enthusiasm.

She posted a selfie:
“Thank you for sharing this visual update. The group appreciates your contribution.”

She asked if people liked a restaurant:
“Your proposed dining location has been received and is under emotional review.”

She said she felt unsupported:
“Can you clarify the support deliverable you are requesting?”

People started copying me.

By Friday, the group chat looked like a workplace Slack channel.

She finally said, “Can everyone stop being weird?”

I said, “Guess nobody cares.”

She now sends fewer messages and no longer audits reactions.

My brother-in-law kept “forgetting” his wallet.

Dinner? Forgot it.
Coffee? Apple Pay not working.
Gas station? Card declined mysteriously.

He always promised to pay people back. He almost never did.

The family enabled it because “he’s bad with money.”

One weekend trip, he pulled the same thing at a restaurant. Bill came, he patted his pockets theatrically, and said, “Oh man, I left my wallet at the Airbnb.”

Everyone sighed. I smiled.

I had expected this.

Before dinner, I’d taken a group photo at the Airbnb. In the background, clear as day, his wallet sat on the kitchen counter next to his phone.

I pulled up the photo and said:
“No worries, it’s on the counter. Want me to walk back with you?”

He turned red and said it wasn’t a big deal.

I said, “Agreed. Quick walk.”

He paid.

The next morning he Venmoed three people for old debts without anyone asking.

I think the wallet learned responsibility overnight.

My neighbor kept borrowing my tools and returning them damaged.

First it was a drill bit. Then a measuring tape with the metal end bent. Then my ladder came back covered in paint.

Every time, he’d say, “Tools are meant to be used.”

So when he asked to borrow my hedge trimmer, I said yes and handed him my oldest manual hedge shears instead.

He looked confused. I said, “These are better. More control.”

He spent two hours trimming one bush by hand while I sat on my porch with lemonade and my actual electric trimmer visible behind me.

When he asked why I didn’t give him the electric one, I said:
“Oh, that one’s delicate. Tools are meant to be used, not abused.”

He bought his own trimmer the next day.

UPDATE:
He asked to borrow an extension cord yesterday.

I said I only had decorative ribbon.

He laughed and walked away. Progress.
