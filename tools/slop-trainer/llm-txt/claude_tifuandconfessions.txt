TIFU by trying to impress my date with a "homemade" dinner.

So I matched with this girl who's a genuinely good cook, posts food on her stories, the whole thing. I, a man whose signature dish is "scrambled eggs but a little sad," panicked and said I'd cook for our third date.

The plan was simple: order from a nice restaurant, plate it myself, claim glory. Foolproof. I ordered the food early, hid the containers, and was mid-transfer onto my own plates when she texted "running early, here in 5!"

In my panic I shoved all the takeout containers into the oven to hide them. Then, to set the mood, I... preheated the oven. For the "dessert" I was also definitely not making.

She arrived to a smoke alarm, a kitchen full of the smell of burning plastic, and me standing there holding a spatula over food I clearly did not make, with the receipt now on fire inside my own oven. The containers melted onto the rack. She watched me pull a flaming plastic blob out with tongs.

She laughed so hard she cried. We've been together eight months now. She still introduces me as "the guy who tried to bake a receipt."

TL;DR: faked a homemade dinner, hid the evidence in an oven, then turned the oven on, and nearly burned down my apartment in front of a woman who could've just had Olive Garden.

TIFU by replying-all to 4,000 people to ask everyone to stop replying-all.

You already know how this ends but let me walk you to your doom alongside me.

Someone at my company sent a company-wide email to the entire org by mistake. Within minutes the replies started: "please remove me from this list," "why am I getting this," "+1 remove me," all sent reply-all to four thousand people. Classic.

I, in my infinite wisdom, decided to be the hero. I crafted a stern, helpful message: "PLEASE stop replying all, you are emailing the entire company." And I sent it. Reply. All.

I became the very thing I sought to destroy. My email kicked off a SECOND wave. People started replying-all to ME to tell me to stop replying-all. The irony was not lost on anyone. Someone made a meme. By the end of the day the email thread had its own nickname. IT eventually had to disable reply-all org-wide and sent a memo that did not name me but absolutely was about me.

My manager called it "a learning experience." A guy from a department I've never interacted with stops me in the hall sometimes just to say "reply all" under his breath.

TL;DR: tried to stop a reply-all storm by adding to the reply-all storm. I am now corporate folklore.

TIFU by "fixing" my grandma's phone.

My grandma called me in a panic because her phone was "broken" — the screen was huge, everything zoomed in, she couldn't read her texts. I'm the family tech support so I drove over feeling very competent and important.

I get there, take her phone, and confidently start fixing it. Turn zoom off. Reset display settings. Nothing works. The screen is still enormous. I'm sweating. I'm googling. I'm starting to think the phone is genuinely broken and I'll have to tell my grandma her phone is dead.

After 45 minutes of me muttering and restarting the phone, my 8-year-old nephew wanders in, glances over my shoulder, and says "she's just got it on the magnifier." He reaches over, double-taps with three fingers, and the screen returns to normal instantly.

A FEATURE. It was an accessibility feature she'd turned on by accident. My nephew, who cannot yet do long division, solved in four seconds what humbled me for nearly an hour. My grandma now tells people I "tried my best." My nephew is the family tech support now. I have been deposed.

TL;DR: spent 45 minutes failing to fix a phone that wasn't broken, got dethroned as family IT by a third grader.

TIFU by lying about knowing how to ride a horse to impress a girl, on a date, at a horse ranch.

She was very into horses. I am a man who has seen horses on television. When she invited me to her family's ranch and asked if I could ride, I said — and I want to be clear there was no reason to say this — "oh yeah, all the time."

They saddled me a horse named Diesel. I want you to understand that Diesel knew. Within ninety seconds Diesel understood that I had never been within ten feet of a horse and decided to test the limits of my soul.

I did not fall off, which is the only reason I'm alive to write this. But I clung to that horse like a koala while it walked, completely calmly, in the exact opposite direction of the group, into a field, and stopped to eat grass for what felt like a geological era. I could not steer. I could not stop. I just sat there, slowly drifting away from the date, yelling "great horse!" while everyone watched.

Her dad had to come get me. On foot. He walked across the field, took Diesel's reins, and led me back like a child on a pony at a fair. He didn't say a word. He didn't have to.

TL;DR: said I could ride a horse, could not ride a horse, was rescued by my date's father in front of God and everyone, and got slowly kidnapped by a horse named Diesel.

TIFU by trying to be quirky and confident at a job interview.

I read one too many articles about "standing out" and "showing personality." So when the interviewer did the classic "so, tell me about yourself," I decided I would NOT give a boring answer. I would be memorable. I would be the candidate they talk about.

I leaned back, smiled, and said "well, my Roman Empire is the fall of the actual Roman Empire."

It was supposed to be a charming little joke. There was a silence. A long one. The interviewer, an older woman with zero internet-meme context, just stared at me and said, slowly, "...are you a history major?"

I am not a history major. I applied for a marketing role. I then had to spend four agonizing minutes explaining the meme, then explaining I don't actually know much about the Roman Empire, then watching her write something down. I have never wanted to leave a room more.

Plot twist: I got the job. My new boss told me weeks later they hired me because "anyone willing to fail that hard and keep talking has good instincts for marketing." So honestly the meme worked, just not for any reason I intended.

TL;DR: opened a job interview with a meme the interviewer didn't know, died inside explaining it, somehow got hired anyway.
